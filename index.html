01 / Foundations

One molecule, several useful views

A molecular structure is more than a drawing: coordinates describe where atoms sit, while a file format determines which chemical details travel with them.

3D

Geometry is a model

Atoms are represented as points or spheres in three-dimensional space. Distances, angles, and torsions define a geometry; a force field or electronic-structure method assigns its energy.

E(x)

Optimization searches

Geometry optimization changes the nuclear coordinates to approach a stationary point on the potential energy surface. A low-energy geometry is method- and environment-dependent.

∠

Coordinates encode shape

Cartesian coordinates store each atom's x, y, z. Internal coordinates use bond lengths, bond angles, and dihedrals—often a more chemically intuitive parameterization.

Familiar geometry snapshots

H₂O: bent, with an experimental gas-phase H–O–H angle near 104.5°.

CO₂: linear, O=C=O, with a 180° angle.

CH₄: tetrahedral, with H–C–H angles near 109.5°.

These are reference-like teaching values; predicted values vary with the model, phase, and conditions.

From model to file

.XYZ is compact and stores atom coordinates but no explicit connectivity. .PDB is widely used for biomolecular structures. .MOL V2000 includes atom and bond blocks.

Always check units, atom order, charge, connectivity, and software-specific conventions when transferring a structure.
02 / Energy minimization

Move downhill on the PES

∇E

Gradient and nuclear forces

The potential energy surface (PES) maps energy against nuclear coordinates. Its gradient describes local energy change; the force on nucleus i is Fᵢ = −∇ᵢE. At a stationary point, the gradient is zero (within numerical tolerance).

H

Minimum or saddle point?

A zero gradient alone does not prove a minimum. The Hessian is the matrix of second derivatives; its eigenvalues (often interpreted through vibrational frequencies) should be positive for a local minimum. A negative curvature indicates a saddle direction.

Newton / L-BFGS

Newton uses curvature information; L-BFGS approximates it with limited memory. Practical packages often use safeguards and line searches.

03 / Internal coordinates

Z-Matrix live builder

Choose a small molecule and edit its defining internal coordinate(s). The builder converts them into Cartesian positions for visualization and file export.

What does a Z-Matrix specify?

The first atom anchors the origin; later atoms are positioned relative to earlier atoms using a bond length r, bond angle θ, and (when needed) dihedral φ. Three atoms define a plane; a fourth atom can be placed using a torsion.

Internal coordinates can decouple overall translation and rotation and make chemically meaningful constraints easier to apply. They do not guarantee independent motions: coordinates may be redundant or ill-conditioned.

Coordinate conventions

Bond lengths are in ångströms; angles and dihedrals are in degrees. The builder uses a fixed atom ordering and a consistent local placement convention. Symmetry can reduce the independent parameters, but its constraints must be chosen deliberately.

A Z-Matrix is a coordinate specification, not a force field. Editing it changes geometry; this page does not calculate a molecular energy from it.

XYZ coordinates
 

            
Line 1 is the atom count; line 2 is a comment; following lines contain element and X/Y/Z coordinates in Å. XYZ does not store explicit bond connectivity or bond orders.

          

          

            
PDB coordinate records
 

            
PDB uses fixed-column ATOM/HETATM records for atom identity, residue details, and X/Y/Z in Å, with optional CONECT records. It has macromolecular heritage and limited bond-order semantics.

          

          

            
MDL MOL · V2000
 

            
A V2000 file contains a header, counts line, atom block, bond block, and terminator. This builder defines a simple connectivity template for each preset and writes explicit single/double bond orders.

          

          

            
Optimization output log · example
 

            
Illustrative log showing energy change, maximum gradient/force, displacement, and convergence status. It is not generated by a quantum chemistry engine.
